Edge Check

Drop in your backtest trades. Find out if your edge is real.

Your trades never leave your computer. Everything runs in your browser.

1. Load your trades

A CSV with one row per trade. From Excel: File → Save As → CSV UTF-8. From Google Sheets: File → Download → CSV.

or drop it here

Starting from nothing? Download the template CSV, fill in your trades, and load it here.

2. Match your columns

We guessed which of your columns is which from the header names. Check each one; the grey line under it shows values from your file.

3. Your stats

Every number is worked out from the trades below, in file order. Tap “What it means” on any card.

Stats card

A square picture of these results (1080 × 1080) to post. It uses the trades in the range above, and your browser paints it: nothing is uploaded.

4. Equity curve

Your running total of R after each trade. The shaded stretch is the max drawdown. Hover or tap the line to read a trade; click it (or tap it twice) to jump to that trade in the table.

5. Splits

The same trades, grouped. The line under each table names the group that made the most and any group that lost money, counting only groups of 5 or more trades. A split is an idea to test, not a new rule: slice enough ways and some group always looks best by luck alone.

6. MAE and MFE

MAE is the most a trade went against you before it closed; MFE is the most it went your way. Both are in R and read as sizes, so −0.16 and 0.16 both mean 0.16R. The winners’ MAE shows whether your stop is too tight; the losers’ MFE shows whether your target is too greedy.
